## Question 2
How can you use B = MAP to make making new habits easier? What are M, A and P?

Behavior = Motivation + Ability + Prompt is represented by B = MAP. M stands for motivation, or the degree to which we wish to engage in the behavior. Ability, represented by the letter A, indicates how simple or complex the behavior is. The likelihood of forming a habit is increased when it is small and simple. P stands for Prompt, a cue or reminder that instructs us to carry out the action.

By simplifying the habit, providing sufficient motivation, and associating it with a dependable prompt, I can implement B = MAP. For instance, rather than committing to exercising for 30 minutes each day, I could commence with two push-ups immediately following my dental hygiene. The habit is effortless to execute after two push-ups, and the prompt is to brush my teeth.

## Question 3
Why it is important to "Shine" or Celebrate after each successful completion of habit? 

The importance of celebrating after successfully completing a habit is that it creates a positive feeling called “Shine.” This positive feeling connects the habit with achievement and makes us want to do it again. If I do a small habit like two push-ups and feel good about having done it right away, I’m forming a positive association with that behavior. This can help reinforce the habit over time.

The important point is that by celebrating small success we build confidence and encourage ourselves to continue the habit. Instead of thinking about how much we did, we can think about the fact that we got the habit.

## Question 5
What is the book's perspective about Identity?

The viewpoint held by the book is that change of habits occurs when there is an identity change, and not just concentrating on the outcome of what we want. For example, we may stop saying "I wish to read more books" but "I am a reader."

Every time we do an action, we cast a vote in favor of the person we want to become. As such, doing things will build our identity. We should therefore concentrate not only on our outcomes but the person we want to be.

## Question 6
Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit easier to do?

According to the book, in order to make a behavior easier, we need to minimize its friction. In other words, the easier and more convenient the behavior is, the more probable it is to perform.

One such technique is called Two-Minute Rule: We begin our habit formation by making the habit tiny enough to take almost no time at all. Instead of committing to reading for half an hour daily, I commit to reading just one page per day.

In addition, we can prepare our environment beforehand. Putting the book either on my desk or by the bedside helps me start reading. As the third law of behavior change in the book states: Make it easy.

## Question 7
Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit harder to do?

To make a bad habit harder to perform, one can add friction between us and the habit, which involves making more actions necessary so that the behavior becomes more complicated.

For instance, if I would like to reduce the amount of time spent online, I may sign out of those applications, delete them from the phone's screen or uninstall the apps. It becomes difficult to access the application, and I get a chance to decide not to perform the behavior.

It is the complete opposite of "Make it easy" and represents the third law of overcoming a bad habit: "Make it difficult."


## Question 8
Pick one habit that you would like to do more of? What are the steps that you can take to make it make the cue obvious or the habit more attractive or easy and or response satisfying?

One habit that I would like to do more often is regular reading. Based on Atomic Habits, a good habit can become more easily formed by making it obvious, attractive, easy, and satisfying.

- Make the cue obvious: Put a book in my desk or next to my bed so that I can see it.
- Make it attractive: Read books about topics I really like.
- Make it easy: Begin by reading 5–10 pages per day instead of setting a big goal.
- Make it satisfying: Note down the days when I read and be happy about my streak.

This will help me form a good reading habit, instead of it being some sort of chore.

## Question 9
Pick one habit that you would like to eliminate or do less of? What are the steps that you can take to make it make the cue invisible or the process unattractive or hard or the response unsatisfying?

The habit that I want to quit or lower is the time that I spend on social media. The way that can be applied to overcome this habit is to go against the four laws of behavior change:

- Make the cue invisible: Uninstall social media apps from the home screen and disable notifications of such applications.
- Make it unattractive: Make it clear to myself that I am just wasting my time scrolling through social media pages.
- Make it difficult: Sign out of the apps or uninstall them completely in order to make using these apps less accessible to me.
- Make it unsatisfying: Set daily time limits of how long I can spend on social media by measuring the time I use these applications.

It will make the behavior change more challenging and therefore will not happen automatically anymore.
